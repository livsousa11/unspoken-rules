The Unofficial Rulebook

These are the little social rules people often expect you to know, even though nobody really explains them.

Rule #1

Don't swipe through someone's photos without asking.

If someone shows you one picture on their phone, stop there unless they tell you to keep looking.

Rule #2

Let people exit before you enter.

When an elevator, train, or doorway opens, let the people inside come out first.

Rule #3

Don't stand too close to strangers.

Give people some personal space when waiting in a line or talking.

Rule #4

Use headphones in public.

Don't play music, videos, or phone calls loudly around other people.

Rule #5

Don't block the whole sidewalk.

If you're walking with a group, leave room for other people to pass.

Rule #6

Give a thank-you wave in traffic.

If another driver lets you merge or gives you space, a small wave is a common way to say thank you.

Rule #7

Don't take the last piece without asking.

If food is being shared, ask before taking the final piece.

Rule #8

Return things you borrow.

Bring borrowed items back in roughly the same condition you received them.

Rule #9

Don't read someone's messages over their shoulder.

Phones and computer screens are generally considered private.

Rule #10

Don't cut in line.

Go to the end of the line unless someone specifically saves you a spot.

Rule #11

Say thank you when someone holds the door.

A quick thank you acknowledges that they made an effort for you.

Rule #12

Don't ask someone why they look tired.

Comments about someone's appearance can make them uncomfortable even when you mean well.

Rule #13

Don't announce someone else's news.

Pregnancies, engagements, new jobs, and other personal news belong to that person to share.

Rule #14

Don't keep someone trapped in a conversation.

If someone keeps looking away, giving short answers, or moving toward the exit, they may be ready to leave.

Rule #15

Clean up after yourself.

In shared spaces, try to leave the area as clean as you found it.

Rule #16

Don't pressure someone who says no.

A no usually means the subject is finished unless they choose to bring it up again.

Rule #17

Don't make plans in front of someone who isn't invited.

It can make the person feel deliberately excluded.

Rule #18

If you're late, let people know.

A quick text helps others know you're still coming.

Rule #19

Don't touch people's belongings without asking.

Even ordinary objects can be personal or important to someone.

Rule #20

Keep shared spaces usable.

Don't spread your belongings across every seat, table, or counter.

Rule #21

Don't interrupt someone repeatedly.

Wait for a natural pause before jumping into the conversation.

Rule #22

Don't correct every small mistake.

If the mistake doesn't really matter, correcting it may be more distracting than helpful.

Rule #23

Ask before putting someone on speakerphone.

They may say something private without realizing other people can hear.

Rule #24

Don't share someone's secret because you think it's harmless.

If they told you privately, assume they expect it to stay private.

Rule #25

Don't ask strangers extremely personal questions.

Money, relationships, medical issues, and family matters can be sensitive topics.

Rule #26

Don't stare.

Briefly noticing someone is normal. Watching them for a long time can feel uncomfortable.

Rule #27

Give people time to answer a text.

Not everyone can respond immediately, even if you can see they were online.

Rule #28

Don't send "???" because someone hasn't replied yet.

Unless something is urgent, repeated messages can feel like pressure.

Rule #29

Don't volunteer someone for something without asking them.

Check with them before promising their time or help.

Rule #30

If someone buys you a meal, say thank you.

You don't have to make a huge deal about it; acknowledging the gesture is enough.

Rule #31

Don't comment on how much someone is eating.

What or how much someone eats is usually their own business.

Rule #32

Don't ask someone if they're pregnant.

Wait for them to tell you themselves.

Rule #33

Don't make fun of someone's laugh.

People can become self-conscious about things they previously enjoyed doing naturally.

Rule #34

When someone is venting, they may not want advice.

Sometimes people want to be heard instead of having the problem solved.

Rule #35

Don't make every conversation about yourself.

Sharing a similar experience can help, but remember to bring the conversation back to the other person too.

Rule #36

Knock before entering a closed bedroom or bathroom.

A closed door usually signals that someone wants privacy.

Rule #37

Don't use someone else's things just because they're nearby.

Ask first, especially with food, chargers, clothing, and personal items.

Rule #38

Don't spoil movies or shows without warning.

Ask whether someone has seen it before revealing important plot details.

Rule #39

Don't repeatedly point out that someone is quiet.

They probably already know and may not want attention drawn to it.

Rule #40

Don't embarrass someone for not knowing something.

If they ask a sincere question, explain it without making them feel foolish.

Rule #41

Don't invite extra people without asking the host.

The host may have planned for a specific number of guests.

Rule #42

Offer to help clean up when you're a guest.

You don't always have to clean, but offering is a polite gesture.

Rule #43

Don't expect an immediate response to every message.

Texting gives people the ability to respond when they have time.

Rule #44

Let people finish telling their story.

Try not to jump ahead and tell them what you think they're about to say.

Rule #45

Don't point out something about someone's appearance they can't quickly fix.

If they can't fix it in a few seconds, mentioning it may only make them self-conscious.

Rule #46

If someone has headphones on, they may not want to talk.

Headphones often signal that someone wants to focus or have some space.

Rule #47

Don't force people to explain why they don't drink or eat something.

A simple "no thank you" should usually be enough.

Rule #48

Don't compare people's bodies.

Even comments meant as compliments can make someone uncomfortable.

Rule #49

Say goodbye before leaving a small gathering.

At smaller events, disappearing without saying anything can feel abrupt.

Rule #50

If you're unsure about a social rule, it's okay to ask.

Unspoken rules aren't actually obvious to everyone. Asking for clarification is completely reasonable.
